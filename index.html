Volt Money: letting demat holders borrow against their mutual funds

Problem

Ankur has about ₹16 lakh in mutual funds, almost all in demat. The app counts only his small non-demat fund, so he can't reach the ₹25,000 minimum loan.

Insight

The reason is buried in a popup, and blocked users never leave reviews. It's a silent drop-off that only funnel data shows.

Solution

Demat pledges through CDSL/NSDL, one combined limit, a clear "why this limit" screen, and servicing fixes drawn from user reviews.

GoodScore

Credit health

Users check their credit score, see it's low, and leave.

  • Insight: a score without a plan feels like a verdict, not a starting point.
  • Fix: a personal 90-day score plan with the top two actions, such as clearing an overdue or cutting card usage below 30%, plus the expected score impact and reminders.
Read the full breakdown

Context

GoodScore helps people track and improve their credit score. Most users arrive to check their score once, often after a loan or card rejection.

Why users drop

A low number with no clear next step feels like a verdict. Users see the problem but not a path, so they leave and rarely come back.

What I would build

  1. Rank the factors pulling the score down (overdue payments, high card usage, frequent loan enquiries) by likely impact.
  2. Show only the top two actions, each with a projected score range, such as "clear this overdue: +20 to 40 points".
  3. Turn it into a 90-day plan with weekly nudges and a progress tracker.
  4. Celebrate the change when the next bureau update lands, and suggest the next action.

How I would measure it

Primary: share of users completing one score action within 30 days. Secondary: 60-day return rate. Guardrail: complaints that projections were misleading.

Trade-off to manage

Projections must be shown as ranges with plain caveats. Over-promising a score jump would win the click and lose trust.

North-star metricUsers completing one score action in 30 days

Upstox

Investing

KYC-complete users never place their first trade.

  • Insight: beginners land on F&O-first screens and a multi-step fund-add flow.
  • Fix: a beginner mode with a guided starter SIP and one-tap UPI fund-add.
Read the full breakdown

Context

Upstox spends heavily to get users through KYC. A user who never places a first trade has cost money and earned nothing.

Why users drop

New investors land on screens built for active traders, with F&O and charts up front, and adding money takes several steps. The first action feels risky and complicated.

What I would build

  1. Ask one question at onboarding ("new to investing?") and switch on a beginner mode.
  2. Lead with a guided starter SIP in a broad index fund at a small amount.
  3. Make fund-add one tap with UPI, pre-filled with the SIP amount.
  4. Show a three-step first-investment checklist, then let users graduate to the full app.

How I would measure it

Primary: KYC-to-first-trade rate. Secondary: time from KYC to first investment. Guardrail: share of new users trading F&O in their first month.

Trade-off to manage

Beginner mode should simplify, not hide. Users must be able to switch to the full app at any time.

North-star metricKYC-to-first-trade rate

True Balance

Lending

New-to-credit borrowers miss their very first repayment.

  • Insight: due dates and amounts are unclear, and autopay setup is easy to skip.
  • Fix: a repayment calendar at disbursal, a plain-language UPI AutoPay nudge, WhatsApp reminders before the due date, and a part-pay option.
Read the full breakdown

Context

True Balance lends small amounts to new-to-credit users. The first repayment shapes both the user's credit history and the loan's profitability.

Why users drop

Due dates and exact amounts are easy to miss, and AutoPay setup is skippable. Many first misses are confusion, not unwillingness to pay.

What I would build

  1. Show a repayment calendar at disbursal with each date and exact amount.
  2. Add a short UPI AutoPay step that explains in plain words what it does and how to cancel it.
  3. Send reminders by app and WhatsApp three days and one day before the due date.
  4. Offer a part-pay option and clear grace-period messaging if the user is short.

How I would measure it

Primary: first repayment paid on time. Secondary: AutoPay adoption at disbursal. Guardrail: disbursal conversion, so the extra step doesn't cost loans.

Trade-off to manage

Making AutoPay mandatory would raise on-time rates but could push away good borrowers. Test it as a strong default first.

North-star metricFirst repayment paid on time

Stable Money

Savings

Users browse high FD rates but hesitate to book with a bank they don't know.

  • Insight: the barrier is trust, not rates.
  • Fix: a trust layer explaining DICGC deposit insurance (up to ₹5 lakh per bank), a suggestion to split across banks to stay covered, and a small first deposit to try it.
Read the full breakdown

Context

Stable Money lists fixed deposits from many banks, including small finance banks that pay higher rates than big banks.

Why users drop

Users compare rates, then hesitate to put money in a bank they have never heard of. The barrier is trust, not price.

What I would build

  1. Add a trust panel to every bank: RBI-regulated status and DICGC deposit insurance of up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank.
  2. Suggest splitting larger amounts across banks so every rupee stays insured.
  3. Let users start with a small first deposit to experience the full journey.
  4. Show maturity value and payout dates clearly before booking.

How I would measure it

Primary: browse-to-first-FD conversion. Secondary: second FD within 90 days. Guardrail: premature withdrawal rate.

Trade-off to manage

Too much reassurance can feel like a warning. Keep the trust layer short, factual and one tap away.

North-star metricBrowse-to-first-FD conversion

PRD

Loans against demat mutual funds

How I turn a case study into something a team can build: scope, requirements, metrics and a rollout plan. A condensed version is below.

Volt Money · Draft v1
16 Oct 2026
1. Problem 2. Goals 3. Requirements 4. Success metrics 5. Rollout

01Problem

User: investors who hold mutual funds in demat get a tiny limit or none at all. Ankur's ₹16 lakh portfolio gives a ₹11,045 limit, below the ₹25,000 minimum.

Business: creditworthy, low-risk borrowers drop off at eligibility. Volt Money is owned by DSP, which gains when investors borrow instead of redeeming.

Root causes: holdings fetch reads only registrar data; pledges support only registrar liens; the low-limit screen has no next step.

02Goals

In scope

  • Count demat mutual funds toward the limit
  • One combined limit across holdings
  • Explain low limits with a fix
  • Fix servicing gaps before larger limits

Not in v1

  • Loans against shares, bonds or ETFs
  • Changing the ₹25,000 minimum
  • A new third-party lender

Software people actually use

I spotted a gap in how a small factory tracked purchases and payments, and built the fix.

In active use

Procurement system, Hard Chrome Tech

Problem: purchasing ran on paper and spreadsheets. Nobody had a reliable view of what was owed to whom, which deliveries were late, or what was being spent.

What I built: one SQL-backed system for the full purchase cycle, from purchase order to payment.

  • Vendors, item master and purchase orders with print-ready POs
  • Goods receipt, invoices, payments and a vendor ledger with CSV export
  • A live dashboard of dues, overdue deliveries and monthly spend

Product decisions: GST splits itself into CGST + SGST or IGST based on the vendor's state; uploaded bills are read automatically but nothing is saved until the user confirms; alerts flag late deliveries and items missing a GST rate.

In use: 9 vendors, 18 items and 8 purchase orders tracked so far.
